# Backfire Awards design guidelines

Set by Erik on October 21, 2026, while redesigning the Moses teardown page. They apply to every page. The palette and type are in `brand/kit.json`; this file covers layout and surfaces.

## 1. Square objects, rounded containers
- **Square:** the things that get shared or exported: carousel cards, feed posts, element tiles, search result cards and product cards. They echo the periodic table and Instagram.
- **Rounded:** everything around them: panels, sections, the hero, dialogs, menus, buttons (pills), chips and form fields.

## 2. Menus and footers are square and run edge to edge
- **Section menu:** a square bar pinned to the top of the window, spanning from the side rail to the right edge. The current section lights orange. On teardowns it also carries Share, Copy link and Comment.
- **Footer:** full width and square, and it sits at the bottom of the window even on short pages.

## 3. Night glass over warm 1970s scenes
- **Background:** a warm 1970s scene behind every page, under a navy wash (about 45 to 75 percent).
- **Teardown pages:** the scene changes only a few times per page, with a slow fade.
- **Panels:** night glass, a translucent navy with blur, with cream text.
- **Category color:** no page is washed in a category's color. Category color lives on the element tile and the carousel only.

## 4. The hero and the first screen
- **Hero:** the compact hero art (`img/brand/hero-compact`) with rounded corners. It shows in full, never cropped, and scrolls away with the page.
- **Teardown pages:** the hero, a title panel in the story column, the section menu, then the carousel.
- **The first screen** is often a visitor's first view of the brand: color, the hero and the carousel, not a gray note.

## 5. Less formatting, less to read
- **Carousel cards:** one statement per card, with the one key line in bold.
- **No duplicate labels:** if the section name or menu already says it, the panel doesn't repeat it.
- **Long data folds:** show four measures first, and keep the dollar figures and gaps closed until opened.

## 6. One accent
Orange (#EE6B3A, with navy text) is the call to action and the "you are here" mark. Other colors identify things (patterns, categories, chart series) and are never used as the action color.
