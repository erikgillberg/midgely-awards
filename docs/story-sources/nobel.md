## Judgment calls

1. **The record's backfire line needed correcting.** It says dynamite was "a much more practical explosive for warfare." Sources say the opposite: dynamite "has never been popular in warfare because it degenerates quickly under severe conditions" (Wikipedia, Dynamite), and armies adopted TNT for shells. The documented backfires are civilian bombing campaigns (dynamite) and Nobel's later military products (ballistite, Bofors). The draft tells it that way. Suggest updating the record's `backfire`.
2. **The record's "his discomfort with that legacy gave us the Nobel Prizes" was cut.** It is a motive claim, and it rests on the "merchant of death" premature obituary, which historians have not found (History.com: "historians have yet to find an original copy"; Wikipedia notes it is questioned). Test 1: no myths. The thanks card states only the documented will.
3. **Pattern: moved.** Making the explosive stable fixed accidental explosions and moved the danger to deliberate use. Alternatives considered: flipped (no teaching intent), surplus (not the mechanism). Midgley also uses "moved"; if the site wants spread across patterns, this is the one to revisit.
4. **Foresight "knowable"**: the card argues from what happened in Nobel's lifetime (bombings from 1881; his own military powder from 1887), not from hindsight. It does not claim what he personally expected in 1867.
5. **Gotthard deaths left out.** The same TRID abstract says more than 300 people died building the tunnel (official counts elsewhere: 177; a historian's revision: 199). Causes were many, not dynamite alone. Listing the travel-time gain without the toll could read as one-sided; a reviewer may want a short mention.
6. **Fenian casualty figure (4 killed, 86 wounded) cut**: uncited in Wikipedia, and Whelehan says few injuries in the 1885 attacks.
7. **Emil's death** is stated once, factually, as the problem being solved. Not used as irony.
8. **Bertha von Suttner quote** ("my factories may make an end of war sooner than your congresses") left out: wording and date could not be verified to a primary source.
9. **Patent date**: English patent May 7, 1867; the Swedish date varies by source (Sept 13 or Oct 19). The card says only 1867.
