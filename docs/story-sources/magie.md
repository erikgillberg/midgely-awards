## Judgment calls

1. **Foresight graded "unforeseeable" to match the record, but a reviewer should look hard.** Magie herself wrote the get-rich rules (the 1904 patent's object is to amass wealth). That the lesson could be missed was arguably knowable. The card says "Not really" and rests on what was unforeseeable: a mass-market bestseller that dropped the argument. If the record moves to `mixed`, retitle to "Partly."
2. **The twist card is the strongest fact and cuts against the simple story.** The 1904 game already rewarded getting rich; the "two rule sets" with a cooperative Prosperity version are documented for the 1932 edition. Some popular accounts imply both rule sets date to 1904; I did not find a primary source for that and did not claim it.
3. **Darrow**: stated only as documented ("who claimed he invented it"). No characterization of him.
4. **$500**: HNN says "for a revised patent"; Wikipedia says Parker bought her 1924 patent. History.com says Parker "offered" $500. Treated as paid per HNN/The New Republic.
5. **The 1979 appeals opinion** (611 F.2d 296) did not return Magie by name in a full-text search, so the card does not say the court named her. It says research for the lawsuit brought her patents back to light.
6. **275 million is Hasbro's own marketing figure** (2015). Labeled as Hasbro's.
7. **Left out**: Mary Pilon's book details and the 1936 Washington Evening Star interview, which I could not verify directly.
