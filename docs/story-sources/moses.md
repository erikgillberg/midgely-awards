## Judgment calls

1. **The 250,000 figure has a scope dispute.** Harvard JCHS summarizes Caro's estimate as about 250,000 across roads, bridges, housing and urban renewal combined. Other secondary sources (99% Invisible, review quotes of the book) say Caro put 250,000 on highways alone and about as many again on urban renewal, roughly 500,000 total. I used the narrower, combined reading so nothing rounds against Moses. A reviewer with the book should check Caro's exact wording (it is in the book's opening chapter).
2. **Pattern: `everyone`.** It captures the induced-demand mechanism (a faster road helps each driver; when everyone drives, it's slow again). It does not capture the displacement harm, which is a separate cost of the same decisions. `moved` was considered and rejected because traffic didn't move; it grew.
3. **Cross Bronx displacement numbers were left out.** Sources range from about 1,500 residents (Wikipedia citing the NYT, 2001) to 1,530 apartments in one section (Caro) to as many as 60,000 (an uncited claim). They conflict, so the card says only that it cut through working-class neighborhoods.
4. **Caro is the main source for the induced-traffic history,** read here through Wikipedia's quotations. I did not open the book.
5. **Left out as contested or personal:** the claim that Long Island parkway bridges were built low to keep buses (and so poorer and Black riders) away from Jones Beach (disputed by later historians); that Moses never drove (personal life); characterizations of his motives or temperament.
6. **Health:** asthma rates near the Cross Bronx are named by officials in the press release, but I didn't find a study attributing them to the expressway, so the ledger says "noise and air pollution, officials say" and doesn't claim asthma.
7. **Ledger balance:** gains carry sourced city figures (NYC Parks); costs carry Caro's estimate and documented congestion. The "13 bridges and 416 miles" count is from one source (TCLF), so it is labeled "by one count."
