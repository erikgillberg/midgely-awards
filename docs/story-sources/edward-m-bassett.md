## Judgment calls

1. **The record calls this "single-use zoning," and the 1916 code wasn't that, strictly.** New York's 1916 resolution separated residence, business and unrestricted districts and limited height. It did not create single-family-only districts. Those came later elsewhere (Berkeley is usually cited for 1916), and the model act Bassett co-wrote in 1924 expressly enabled them. The carousel credits Bassett with the 1916 code and the 1924 model act, and puts the one-family districts in the model act's own words. It never says he invented single-family zoning. Please check this against the record's `solved` and `backfire` text, which is broader than what I could source to Bassett personally.
2. **Causation is shared.** The EPA lists many causes of separated, car-dependent development (incomes, preferences, highway, housing and tax policy). The White House names zoning as one cause of high prices, and the 1.5 million figure is a Moody's estimate. The cards attribute each claim to its source and don't say zoning alone caused either.
3. **Pattern: `later` vs. `stable`.** I chose `later`: the benefit (light, air, homes safe from factories) was immediate, and the cost grew over decades as cities expanded under the rules. `stable` is a close second. The 1924 model act's protest clause (20% of affected owners can force a three-fourths vote) was written so the code would hold, and its own notes say this "tended to stabilize the ordinance." The mirror (a sign-off rule that pays now and costs later as the team grows) fits `later`.
4. **Foresight "Partly."** The knowable part is Judge Westenhaver's 1924 warning about income segregation, overruled in 1926. The unforeseeable part is the car-age sprawl, supported by EPA's description of compact, walkable early-1900s cities. Westenhaver's ruling was about Euclid, Ohio, not Bassett's code.
5. **Quote length.** The judge's quote is 11 words from a public-domain court opinion. CourtListener confirms the text up to "according to their income or"; other sources give the end as "situation in life." The card stops at "income."
6. **Not used:** the Supreme Court's "mere parasite" line about apartments in Euclid (strong, but it is the Court's view, not Bassett's); Minneapolis's 2018 to 2019 reform (sources disagree on the adoption date and on the share of land affected, 70% vs 75%); the claim that DCP's offices are in the Equitable Building (true per the 2016 AIA essay, but currency unverified).
7. **Some pages were read through search excerpts** because they returned 403: the NYC Zoning Handbook PDF (height and setback wording) and the Supreme Court decision page. Everything else in the table was fetched directly.
