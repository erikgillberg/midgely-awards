## Judgment calls

1. **Pattern: surplus.** Kept from the existing haber.json. The shortage (fixed nitrogen) was solved so well that the extra now goes into water and air. "Moved" was the alternative; surplus fits better because the harm is the excess, not a displaced problem.
2. **"About half the world."** This is a modeled estimate (Erisman 2008: 48% in 2008; Smil: about 40% in 2000), always labeled "one estimate." It is an estimate of dependence, not a count.
3. **The "17 in 100" stat compares two different things**: Haber-Bosch nitrogen used on farms (100 Tg) and all nitrogen people ate in food (17 Tg). Erisman uses this comparison to show low nitrogen-use efficiency. The label states both sides; a reviewer may prefer to cut the stat and keep the 4,402 sq mi dead zone instead.
4. **Left out: Haber's chlorine gas work at Ypres (1915).** Documented, but it is a separate decision from the nominated invention. Including it would turn the card into a verdict on the man. Flag for Erik: some readers will find "We owe him one" jarring given that history. Option: retitle the thanks card "Credit where due."
5. **Left out: Erisman's statements about Haber's motivation** ("German patriot," "keen to develop explosives"). Motive claims are against the voice rules. The twist card states only what the ammonia was used for.
6. **Left out: Erisman's "100 to 150 million deaths in armed conflicts" linked to Haber-Bosch nitrogen.** A sweeping attribution, and a death count on a card risks reading as a punchline.
7. **Crookes 1898**: sourced through a secondary account that cites the address; the primary text is on JSTOR. Low risk.
8. **Dead zone image**: the existing NASA image is from 2023 and shows sediment and phytoplankton, not the hypoxic zone itself. The alt text describes it honestly; the card text does not claim the image shows the dead zone.
9. **Old shape vs. model**: the old haber.json used kinds `gotright` and labels like "What went oops"; the draft uses the model's kinds and labels.
