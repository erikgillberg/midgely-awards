## Judgment calls

1. **Who is named.** The record names "Ferdinand Silcox and the US Forest Service." Silcox signed the letter and argued for fire elimination after 1910, so a person is fair to name, but the policy was recommended unanimously by 20 senior staff and the idea that fast attack pays (Greeley 1926, Show and Kotok) predates him. The thanks card says so.
2. **The policy is one slice of fire exclusion.** The Forest Service itself dates fire exclusion to about 1911, and the abstracts of Hagmann et al., Ecological Applications 31(8):e02431 (2021), and Marlon et al., PNAS (2012), attribute the western fire deficit to colonization, land use and suppression together, with climate. The cards say "fuel piled up" without claiming the 1935 letter alone caused it, and the ledger names climate and development.
3. **Numbers not comparable across 1983.** NIFC's pre-1983 acres (52 million in 1930) are not used on any card. The impacts file stores them, flagged.
4. **Oops stat is computed.** Ten-year averages from NIFC, our arithmetic. A reviewer may prefer a single official figure.
5. **Status unfolding.** The fire deficit is still growing, per the Marlon et al. (2012) abstract, and the 2025 to 2026 return to full suppression language is live policy.
6. **Left out:** the 1988 Yellowstone fires and 2000 Cerro Grande (Los Alamos) escape, which cut the other way (let-burn and prescribed fire gone wrong); Smokey Bear on a card, because the 2001 slogan change is officially explained by "the increasing number of wildfires," not by a change of view on fire, and Smokey's image is a protected mark.
7. **Dixie Fire image.** Illustrates scale only. The card does not claim the Dixie Fire was caused by fuel buildup.
