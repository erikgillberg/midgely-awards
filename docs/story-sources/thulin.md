## Judgment calls

1. **Who invented it.** The US patent names only Thulin, and Guinness, Wikipedia (English) and most coverage credit him. But a 2015 Norrkopings Tidningar piece quotes the production manager of the time saying the bag was made in CEO Curt Lindquist's basement, and cites a 2004 company history saying Lindquist filed the patent in Thulin's name. The Swedish inventors' museum repeats this ("it is said"). English Wikipedia's Plastic bag article now credits Lindquist, citing Nils Johansson in Environment and History (2025), which I did not read. The cards say "a small team at Celloplast" and "the patent named Thulin," which all sources support. If Erik wants a person at the center, the nominee could become Celloplast itself.
2. **The turn of the story is attributed, not asserted.** The only direct quote from Raoul Thulin (Independent, from the BBC interview) is about reuse: the folded bag in the pocket, taking it back to the shop, "bizarre" to throw away. The forest motive appears only as the reporter's framing and the BBC's "his design was supposed to help the planet, according to the family." No source from the 1960s says Celloplast designed the bag to save trees, and Lovefors told NT there was "no strategy" behind it, "all chance." The twist card quotes the son on reuse and attributes the planet claim to the family. The headline does not depend on it.
3. **Pattern: everyone, not flipped.** Flipped needs a fix with a message; a bag has none. Everyone fits: each store and shopper rationally takes a free bag, and the total fills a shared commons. Charges fixed it by pricing each choice. Stable is kept third, since baekeland already carries stable as its main pattern for plastics.
4. **Ledger fairness.** The Environment Agency life cycle numbers go on the gains side: by climate impact, the plastic bag beats paper used fewer than 3 times and cotton used fewer than 131 times. The same study says it did not assess litter, which is the harm here, so both columns hold.
5. **Death year.** NT (which says it drew on population register data) and Guinness say 2006; the Swedish inventors' museum gives 8 January 1992. Record uses 2006 and flags it.
6. **Left out:** NT's details on Thulin's marriages, his second wife's profession and a colleague's description of his temperament (private life and character). The Independent's "1,000 years to degrade" and "marine plastic to outweigh fish by 2050" (not verified, and the second is a contested projection). The UN "1 to 5 trillion bags a year" figure, found only in press summaries of a 2018 UNEP report I could not open; Eurostat's EU count is used instead.
7. **Mola mola image** is used on the foresight card because NOAA's caption ties floating bags to jellyfish-eating animals; the card text does not claim this fish ate a bag.
