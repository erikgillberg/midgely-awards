## Judgment calls
1. **Attribution.** Groundwater loss in northwest India has many causes, including state power subsidies, procurement prices and rice (which is not Borlaug's crop). The cards tie the harm to "the farming boom" and to policy, and the thanks card quotes Pingali's finding that policy, not the seeds themselves, drove the overuse. A reviewer should check that the cover does not read as "Borlaug drained the aquifer."
2. **Pattern.** I chose `later` over `everyone`. Pumping is also a commons problem, but what drives this story is a seasonal gain paid for by a decades-long drawdown. Fertilizer runoff could fit `surplus`. I left it as one ledger line.
3. **Foresight.** The record says `mixed`, and the card says "Partly." Borlaug's own 1970 warning was about population, not water, and the card says so instead of treating it as foresight about aquifers.
4. **Left out.** Monoculture and genetic diversity loss, and the claim that Borlaug saved "a billion lives": no primary source supports a number I would print. The India 1965 to 1970 production figures also differ between secondary sources.
5. The 2010 Columbia figures are a decade and a half old. Newer Central Ground Water Board numbers would strengthen the oops card if Erik wants a refresh.
6. "Feeding the wheat more fertilizer was no answer" is a paraphrase of the Nobel speech's description of tall varieties snapping.
