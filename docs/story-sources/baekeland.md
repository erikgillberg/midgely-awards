## Judgment calls

1. **Pattern: `stable`.** Bakelite's selling point was that it never melts or dissolves; the backfire is plastic persisting. `later` (cheap now, paid later) was the runner-up.
2. **Attribution of the plastics age to Baekeland.** Bakelite is a thermoset and is a small share of today's waste; most waste is polyethylene, PET, polypropylene and other thermoplastics invented later. The copy says "Bakelite was first" and "the plastics that came after it," and never says Bakelite itself fills oceans. A reviewer should check this framing is fair.
3. **Foresight "Nothing in 1907 pointed here."** That's a judgment. It rests on production being about 2 Mt a year in 1950 and ocean debris first noticed in the 1960s. It matches the record's `unforeseeable`.
4. **Health effects of microplastics are not claimed.** The Leslie study says whether exposure is a health risk is still undetermined, so the card states only detection. No harm to people is claimed.
5. **The "first to coin microplastics" claim** rests mostly on Thompson's own account and secondary sources.
6. **Treaty status.** The IISD page returned 403 when fetched; the 2024 no-deal and 2026 chair election are from search excerpts of IISD and UNEP pages. Re-check the status before publishing, since talks may have moved.
7. **Shellac "limited supply"** is from Wikipedia's History section (pointer only). The Science History Institute supports the shellac motive but not the word "limited."
8. **Left out:** the Velox sale to Kodak (reputed $750,000; not needed); "material of a thousand uses" tagline (Wikipedia only); the claim shellac melts at about 75 °C (tagged citation needed on Wikipedia).
