## Judgment calls
1. **Foresight vs. record.** The record says `unforeseeable`, and the card says the eggshell harm was not foreseeable. That is honest for the main harm, but not for everything: the US Fish and Wildlife Service published Circular 11 on DDT and wildlife in 1946 (a USFWS release of August 22, 1945 reportedly warned of wildlife damage; I saw it only quoted in search results, so it is unverified), and housefly resistance was reported from 1946 to 1948 (dates vary by source). A reviewer may prefer `mixed`. I kept `unforeseeable` because the card scale renders from the record, and I named the 1946 study on the card so it doesn't overstate the case.
2. **Naples.** A historian (Bailey, Oxford; https://www.history.ox.ac.uk/node/2490476, seen in search results only) argues the Anglo-American account of a quick victory won by DDT leaves things out. So the card says new cases "fell off" and does not claim DDT alone ended the epidemic.
3. **Pattern.** I chose `stable` over `resistance`. DDT's selling point was that it lasted (sprayed once or twice a year instead of weekly), and that same property is what harmed birds. Mosquito resistance appears as a secondary "also" line.
4. **Left out.** Sri Lanka malaria figures (2.8 million to 17 cases): the 1963 low is in an official Sri Lankan bulletin, but I could not trace the baseline and resurgence numbers to a primary source, and the resurgence is tied up in a contested "DDT ban" myth. Also left out are human health effects of DDT, because EPA describes them only as "potential human health risks."
5. **Silent Spring.** Not on the cards, to save space. EPA's page supports a line about it if wanted.
6. "Lasted six months or more" comes from "semestrial or annual applications," which means every six or twelve months.
