## Judgment calls

1. **Pattern: "Chasing the wrong number."** The ranking optimized countable proxies (comments, reshares) for "meaningful social interactions"; anger scores on the same proxies. "The reward gets gamed" also fits (publishers and parties gaming the feed), but the root decision was which number to rank by, so metric is the closer mechanism.
2. **Scope narrowed to the News Feed and the 2018 ranking change.** The record lists privacy loss, addiction and misinformation broadly. I left out Cambridge Analytica and the 2019 $5 billion FTC penalty (a privacy decision, a different story), Myanmar (moderation more than ranking, and involves mass violence; hard to treat in a card without risking test 4), and the January 2025 end of US fact-checking. A reviewer may want one privacy line in the ledger.
3. **The 80% stat is one Polish party's self-estimate, quoted in an internal Facebook report,** not a measured platform-wide number. The stat label says "by its own estimate." It is vivid but thin; a reviewer may prefer no headline stat.
4. **WSJ is paywalled;** quotes are verified through CJR and the Washington Post, both of which reviewed or quoted the documents. The WSJ article is cited by title only; its URL and the Science DOIs were not verified.
5. **Left out on fairness grounds:** WSJ's report, from the memos, that Zuckerberg resisted some proposed fixes because they might reduce engagement. It is a documented report about a decision, but Meta disputes the framing and the card could not hold both sides. A reviewer may want it in the foresight or cleanup card, paired with Meta's response.
6. **Teen body image line is Instagram, not News Feed,** and Meta disputes the reading; it is included as an engagement-ranked feed cost with Meta's own caveat ("not causal"). Cut it if the reviewer wants the ledger strictly on the News Feed.
7. **Foresight is "contested," per the record.** The card separates what Facebook's researchers saw internally (by 2019) from the outside science, which is mixed (Guess 2023 vs the 2024 Science letter).
8. The twist quotes Zuckerberg's stated goal (well-being) rather than imputing any motive.
