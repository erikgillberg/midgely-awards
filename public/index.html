What makes a good nominee · 2 of 3

It caused harm somewhere else

The damage landed on people or places the inventors weren't thinking about. Leaded gasoline made engines run better, and the lead ended up in children's blood.

What makes a good nominee · 3 of 3

Say what people knew

Could anyone have seen the harm coming at the time? Midgley's lead hazard was known. The damage Freon did to the ozone layer wasn't understood until 1974.

The gender gap

Most nominees are men

We estimate women make up only 2 to 5 percent of people who qualify for a Midgley. For most of history, women were kept out of the places where these backfires start: industrial labs, scientific academies, and high-level military and economic planning. Women who did hold power, such as queens and empresses, mostly made local political or military mistakes, not world-scale technical ones.

We want a future where women have every opportunity to win a Midgley. Some say that future would have fewer backfires. We would like to find out. Meet Lizzie Magie.
