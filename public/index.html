The first Midgley Award

Thomas Midgley Jr., 1889 to 1944

In the 1920s he invented leaded gasoline and Freon. Both solved real engineering problems. Leaded gasoline spread lead pollution around the world, and Freon damaged the ozone layer. Our award for backfires at world scale, the Midgley, is named after him. He received the first one.

What this is

A list of inventions and decisions that solved one problem and caused another. You vote on them, argue about them in the comments, and add your own.

  • The Midgley goes to backfires at world scale: leaded gasoline, plastics, the highway that split a city.
  • The Murphy goes to the everyday kind: the office rule that made meetings longer. Share yours.

What makes a good nominee

  • It worked. The idea was a clever, successful answer to a real problem.
  • It caused harm somewhere else. The damage landed on people or places the inventors weren't thinking about. Leaded gasoline made engines run better, and the lead ended up in children's blood.
  • Say what people knew. Could anyone have seen the harm coming at the time? Midgley's lead hazard was known. The ozone damage from Freon wasn't understood until 1974.
