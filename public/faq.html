What is the Backfire Awards?

Teardowns of historic backfires: famous fixes that worked, then caused a new problem somewhere else. Each teardown credits what the fix got right, follows what went wrong, and names the pattern behind it, so founders and inventors can spot the next one before they ship it. The Midgley goes to backfires at world scale.

What makes a good nominee?

  1. It worked. The idea was a clever, successful answer to a real problem. A bad product that never worked doesn't count.
  2. It caused harm somewhere else. The damage landed on people or places the inventors weren't thinking about. Leaded gasoline made engines run better, and the lead ended up in children's blood.
  3. We say what people knew. Could anyone have seen the harm coming at the time? Midgley's lead hazard was known. The damage Freon did to the ozone layer wasn't understood until 1974.

The full standard is in the community guidelines.

How do scores work?

Every nominee gets two scores, side by side: the Oops Score from the judges and the Facepalm Index from readers.

Oops Score

The Backfire Judges (Erik, Spencer, Ritchie, David and Susanna) score each nominee from 1 to 100 with the same four questions. A nominee first has to pass one test: it worked. It solved the problem it was built for. Ideas that never worked are not scored.

Each judge adds up the four, then may move their total by up to 10 points either way, with a one-sentence reason. That is the common sense check, for the things a table misses. Totals stay between 1 and 100. The Oops Score is the average of the judges' totals. It appears once at least two judges have scored a nominee.

What people knew at the time is not part of the score. It is part of the story: every nominee's write-up says whether the harm was knowable, and you can weigh that when you vote.

  • Certified Backfire 90 to 100
  • Big Oops 60 to 89
  • Mostly Fine under 60

Facepalm Index

The Facepalm Index comes from readers. On every nominee you can answer one question: worth it? Tap 💡 for yes or 🤦 for no. The Facepalm Index is the share of readers who said no. It appears once ten people have voted. You can change your vote any time.

Why two scores

The judges and the readers do not always agree. That is part of the fun. A nominee can be a Certified Backfire with a low Facepalm Index, or the other way around.

What will win a Midgley thirty years from now?

That is the serious question behind the joke. Better to try and fail than never try. Mostly. Next we will tear down products and decisions being made today and read them against the patterns. It starts with knowing the patterns.

Why are most nominees men?

We estimate women make up only 2 to 5 percent of people who qualify for a Midgley. For most of history, women were kept out of the places where these backfires start: industrial labs, scientific academies, and high-level military and economic planning. Women who did hold power, such as queens and empresses, mostly made local political or military mistakes, not world-scale technical ones.

We want a future where women have every opportunity to win a Midgley. Some say that future would have fewer backfires. We would like to find out. Meet Lizzie Magie.
