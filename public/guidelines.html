Community guidelines

The Backfire Awards recognize solutions that worked very well at the problem they were built for, and created new problems somewhere else. The site tears them down. Readers suggest new ones and vote on whether each was worth it. These guidelines explain what qualifies and what we publish.

What qualifies

  • A real problem, solved well. The original idea has to be a clever and successful answer to a real problem. A product that was simply bad does not qualify.
  • A new problem somewhere else. The harm landed on people or places the inventors or decision makers were not thinking about.
  • An honest account of what was known. Say whether the harm was knowable at the time, unforeseeable, partly knowable, or still contested.

Not a fit, and why

Some things look like backfires but miss the standard: they never worked, the harm was the point, or the story is really about a lie or an accident. These come up often.

Suggesting a backfire

Tell us the decision or invention, the problem it solved, and the problem it created, with sources. Stick to effects that are documented. When the evidence is still debated, say so. If someone is working on a fix, such as a treaty, a reform or an organization, mention it. The best way to suggest one is to reply to any issue of the Backfire Newsletter.

Every teardown is tagged with a field (energy, cities, food and so on), a status, and the patterns behind it.

People in the teardowns

Many nominees are real people, and some are alive. We tear down the decision and its effects. Leave out claims about a person's motives, character or private life. We only include people for public work, such as inventions, products, policies and plans. Private individuals are never nominees.

Comments and corrections

Comments are paused for now. Discussion happens on our social posts, where the same rules apply: no personal attacks, slurs, threats or harassment, no personal information about anyone, no spam, and nothing illegal to publish.

To ask for a correction or a removal, use the Report button on the entry, or reply to the newsletter.
